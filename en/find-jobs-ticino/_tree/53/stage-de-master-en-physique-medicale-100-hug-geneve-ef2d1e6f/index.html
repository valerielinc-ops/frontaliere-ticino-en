Company description

With more than 13,000 employees representing 160 trades, the University Hospitals of Geneva (HUG) is a national and international reference institution. To learn more about our institution, take a few minutes to view our 2025 retrospective by clicking here . The medical physics group is in the diagnostic department (DDIAG) of the HUG. DDIAG's mission is to provide diagnostics, therapeutic products, therapeutic procedures, and specialized, high-quality medical clinical consultations, as soon as possible and at an acceptable cost, to guide patient care and contribute to therapeutic decisions and actions. The DDIAG Medical Physics Group is active in the medical application of ionizing radiation. Its mission is to ensure the safety of employees who are occupationally exposed to radiation and HUG patients. Examinations using ionizing radiation (e.g. radio-guided procedures, scanners, etc.) can expose patients and medical personnel present in the room significantly. As a result, the group is strongly involved in optimizing the exposure of patients and medical personnel.

Description of post

The position is part of the DDIAG Medical Physics Group, composed of five medical physicists. Your work mission is part of a project to put in place a follow-up protocol for patients who have received a skin dose during radiology or interventional cardiology that may induce deterministic effects. Indeed, from a certain dose threshold, tissue reactions may occur, ranging from transient erythema to more severe skin lesions (localized alopecia, dry or wet desquamation, or even necrosis in extreme cases). This course is a continuation of a previous master's project and will aim to continue and deepen the work already carried out. During your internship, you estimate the maximum dose received by the skin from the data provided by the interventional imaging facilities in order to better identify patients at risk and initiate appropriate clinical follow-up. For this, you perform measurements in the intervention room as well as calculations based on Monte Carlo methods. You have strong knowledge of dosimetry applied to the medical field (this knowledge can also be acquired at the beginning of your master's degree).

Qualifications

You are in the course of a Master's degree (preferably 2nd year) and you are looking for a compulsory and agreed internship in the field of medical physics to validate your university degree. You have knowledge of radiation protection and medical physics and you are autonomous with a good ability to adapt to a multidisciplinary team (physicists and physicists, technicians and technicians, doctors, nurses, etc.). Your knowledge of the Swiss hospital community is an asset. You have an excellent relationship. You have or have a very good level of oral and written French, as well as a good level of English.
