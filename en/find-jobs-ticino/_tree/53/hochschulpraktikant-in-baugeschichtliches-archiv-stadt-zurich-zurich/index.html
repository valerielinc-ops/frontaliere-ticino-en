Full description

For the development of photographs from a significant historical collection, we are looking for 1. March 2027 or by appointment for 9-12 months a dedicated team member.

  • You import digital copies into the database.
  • They research the necessary information to describe the images.
  • They collect the metadata about the images in Excel lists.
  • You can access historical photographs in the image database.
  • They support us in conservation work on historical photographs.
  • You take on tasks in the reading room and in the processing of customer inquiries.
  • Ongoing or completed Bachelor's or Master's degree in Information or Social Sciences (degree a maximum of 12 months ago)
  • Interest in processing and communicating cultural historical content
  • Precise and careful working methods
  • Local knowledge and basic knowledge on the historical development of the city of Zurich
  • Willingness to learn to read old manuscripts
  • Interest in photography and historical imagery
  • Innovative, winning, team capable and resilient personality

An instructive internship, diverse activities and exciting insights into the largest city administration in Switzerland await you. In addition, you will become part of the community for university interns in the city of Zurich, benefit from attractive employment conditions, good social benefits, targeted education and training and networking opportunities, flexible forms of work, as well as a centrally located workplace.

The Office for Urban Development develops the foundations for Zurich’s future in terms of construction, develops urban development strategies for spatial development and coordinates plans for a high-quality urban city worth living in. The Baugeschichtliche Archiv (BAZ) is part of the Office for Urban Development. The BAZ is the right address for anyone interested in the structural development of the city of Zurich. The FOZ collects photographs of buildings, streets and squares in the city of Zurich, accesses them in a database and makes them available to the public. The collection is continuously supplemented with new recordings. It extends from the middle of the 19th century to the present. The BAZ conveys its pictures via the e-pics platform. The online offerings are constantly being expanded.
