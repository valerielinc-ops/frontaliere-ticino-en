Scientific Software Engineer (Weather Warning Verification)

80%-100%, Locarno, fixed-term

The Center for Climate Systems Modeling (C2SM) at ETH Zurich and the Federal Office of Meteorology and Climatology (MeteoSwiss) are collaborating to extend and strengthen MeteoSwiss’s operational warning verification activities. Weather warnings are among the most important services a national meteorological service provides. They help authorities, emergency services and the public prepare for heavy rain, strong winds, thunderstorms, snow, heat and other hazardous weather. To keep improving these warnings and to transparently show how well they perform, we need systematic, objective and reliable verification.

Project background

The project builds on a Python-based verification system that evaluates issued weather warnings and automated warning proposals against observations, such as gridded products and station measurements. A first set of rain warning verification products is currently being made operational with further weather hazard products to follow.

Job description

We are looking for a Scientific Software Engineer to join the Warning & Impact solution team within the Development of Forecasting division of MeteoSwiss for a three-year project on warning verification. Working closely with scientists, developers, forecasters and other users, you will further develop existing verification methods and turn them into robust, maintainable operational code.

Initially, your tasks will be to

  • Improve and extend the operational rain warning verification products
  • Further develop and operationalize the wind warning verification pipeline, extending the existing station-based approach to a grid-based one

Later, depending on the team's priorities and progress, your work will include tasks such as:

  • Integrating an existing nowcasting warning verification tool into our operational system, harmonizing approaches and consolidating code where reasonable
  • Developing snow and heat warning verification pipelines and, if their quality is sufficient, implementing them operationally

The position is limited to three years.

Profile

We welcome applications from candidates with diverse backgrounds who meet most (not necessarily all) of the following criteria:

  • MSc or PhD in computer science, data science, natural sciences (e.g. physics, atmospheric or environmental sciences), or a related field
  • Experience in scientific and operational software development (e.g. automated testing, version control, CI/CD) and a keen interest in writing maintainable, well-documented code
  • Strong Python skills, ideally with experience in handling station and gridded meteorological data, and familiarity with domain-specific formats and frameworks (e.g. Polars, Xarray, earthkit, GRIB, netCDF)
  • Solid understanding of meteorology, preferably with a focus on high-impact weather and weather warnings
  • Familiarity with forecast verification methods and statistical evaluation
  • Willingness to contribute across the full workflow, from turning scientific methods into operational products to maintaining them
  • A collaborative mindset, with the ability to take ownership of tasks and communicate effectively within a team
  • Enjoyment of working closely with forecasters and other users and translating their needs into useful products

We offer

  • The chance to turn approaches for verifying weather warnings into robust operational products used across MeteoSwiss
  • Access to a modern scientific software stack, powerful on-prem and cloud computing platforms and AI-powered tools
  • Close collaboration with forecasters and other users, whose feedback directly shapes the products you build
  • A supportive and interdisciplinary team at Switzerland's national weather service
  • Flexible working arrangements with the possibility of partial home office, and the choice between two main workplace locations: Locarno-Monti or Zurich Airport

Do I need the G permit to apply for this role in Ticino as an Italian cross-border worker?

Yes. For EU/EFTA citizens the G permit no longer depends on a border zone: you need residence in an EU/EFTA state, a Swiss contract and a return home at least once a week. The 20 km band matters for taxation, not for the permit. The Swiss employer files the G-permit application at the Ticino cantonal migration office after the contract is signed — first issuance takes 2-6 weeks. The permit lasts five years with an open-ended contract or one over a year, otherwise for the length of the contract.
