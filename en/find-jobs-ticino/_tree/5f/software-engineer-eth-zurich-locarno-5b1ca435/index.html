The employer is seeking a

Scientific Software Engineer (Meteorological Alert Verification) (identified role)

Responsibilities

  • Improve and expand operational products for the verification of rainfall alerts.
  • Further develop and operationalize the verification system for wind alerts, transitioning from the current station-based approach to a grid-based one.

In a subsequent phase, depending on team priorities and progress, your work will include tasks such as:

  • Integrating an existing tool for the verification of nowcasting (very short-term forecasting) alerts into our operational system, harmonizing approaches and consolidating code where appropriate.
  • Developing verification systems for snow and heat alerts and, provided their quality is sufficient, implementing them operationally.
  • This is a fixed-term position with a duration of three years. Your Profile
  • We welcome applications from diverse backgrounds, provided candidates meet most (though not necessarily all) of the following:

  • Master’s degree (MSc) or PhD in Computer Science, Data Science, Natural Sciences (e.g., Physics, Atmospheric or Environmental Sciences), or a related field
  • Experience in developing scientific and operational software (e.g., automated testing, version control, CI/CD) and a strong interest in writing maintainable, well-documented code
  • Solid proficiency in Python, ideally with experience handling meteorological data (both point-based and gridded) and familiarity with industry-specific formats and frameworks (e.g., Polars, Xarray, Earthkit, GRIB, netCDF)
  • A sound understanding of meteorology, with a particular interest in high-impact weather phenomena and weather warning systems
  • Familiarity with forecast verification methods and statistical evaluation
  • Willingness to contribute to the entire workflow—from translating scientific methods into operational products to their ongoing maintenance
  • A collaborative mindset, a strong sense of personal responsibility, and the ability to communicate effectively within a team
  • Enthusiasm for working closely with operational meteorologists and other users to translate their needs into practical, useful products
  • good knowledge of Italian and excellent knowledge of English

Do I need the G permit to apply for this role in Ticino as an Italian cross-border worker?

Yes. For EU/EFTA citizens the G permit no longer depends on a border zone: you need residence in an EU/EFTA state, a Swiss contract and a return home at least once a week. The 20 km band matters for taxation, not for the permit. The Swiss employer files the G-permit application at the Ticino cantonal migration office after the contract is signed — first issuance takes 2-6 weeks. The permit lasts five years with an open-ended contract or one over a year, otherwise for the length of the contract.
