Full description

Key responsibilities This role offers an opportunity to develop comprehensive factual records and narratives, and support the handling of regulatory, government, and civil litigation matters across a global financial institution. We are looking for an Associate Director to join UBS's Fact Finding Unit within Global Litigation, based in Switzerland. You will develop factual records and narratives across regulatory inquiries, criminal matters, and civil litigation, and conduct cross-border discovery and document productions involving foreign jurisdictions. Discovery tools, document review, and AI-assisted analysis will support that work. You will:

  • Conduct and lead thorough factual investigations, developing comprehensive factual records and narratives to support litigation strategy and regulatory response.
  • Support lead counsel on civil litigations and other civil matters with fact-finding as required during the lifecycle of the matter.
  • Conduct factual development for regulatory inquiries, enforcement investigations, criminal matters and other similar matters involving Switzerland and other relevant jurisdictions, using discovery tools and document review as needed.
  • Use generative AI tools—such as Harvey, Co-Pilot, and Relativity aiR—to support factual investigations and document review, enhance consistency, and apply Technology-Assisted Review (TAR)/Continuous Active Learning (CAL) workflows where appropriate
  • Participate in large-scale document reviews and productions involving foreign jurisdictions, ensuring compliance with Swiss legal requirements (including blocking statutes), data-protection and secrecy obligations, and applicable cross-border protocols
  • Conduct document review, redaction and production for smaller scale cross-border discovery matters.

The team The Fact Finding Unit sits within Global Litigation, part of UBS's Group Legal function, and supports matters across all regions and business divisions. The unit supports litigation, regulatory, enforcement, and other high-risk matters by developing factual records, managing discovery and document review processes, organizing evidence, and leveraging legal technology and AI tools to support fact development.

Your skills and experience

  • Law degree from a recognized Swiss or European university; admitted to practice or qualified to advise on Swiss legal matters
  • 2-3+ years of experience in litigation, regulatory investigations, factual development, or cross-border discovery, with significant exposure to Swiss proceedings
  • Knowledge of Swiss review processes, foreign discovery protocols, and cross-border legal assistance frameworks
  • Hands-on experience with eDiscovery platforms (Relativity strongly preferred)
  • Demonstrated proficiency or strong interest in practical legal technology tools, including generative AI (e.g., Harvey, Co-Pilot, Relativity aiR), used to support factual analysis and review
  • Experience in participating in factual development and review teams and ensuring quality, defensibility, and consistency
  • Fluency in English and German or French and/or Italian
  • Strong understanding of Swiss banking regulations, secrecy obligations, and data protection requirements (FADP/nFADP)
  • Excellent analytical and communication skills, with the ability to distill complex cross-border factual scenarios into clear factual records and narratives
  • Financial services industry experience preferred
  • Excellent communication and stakeholder management skills, with the ability to work across time zones and cultures
  • Self-starter mentality with the ability to operate independently while remaining closely integrated with a global team

Do I need the G permit to apply for this role in Ticino as an Italian cross-border worker?

Yes. For EU/EFTA citizens the G permit no longer depends on a border zone: you need residence in an EU/EFTA state, a Swiss contract and a return home at least once a week. The 20 km band matters for taxation, not for the permit. The Swiss employer files the G-permit application at the Ticino cantonal migration office after the contract is signed — first issuance takes 2-6 weeks. The permit lasts five years with an open-ended contract or one over a year, otherwise for the length of the contract.
