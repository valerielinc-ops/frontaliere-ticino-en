Full description

The Investment Risk Manager is responsible for monitoring and assessing the risks arising from the Bank’s investment and asset management activities, ensuring that portfolios are managed in line with their investment mandates, internal policies and applicable regulatory requirements.

This role involves working closely with Portfolio Managers and other Bank departments, providing independent risk assessments, monitoring portfolio exposures and ensuring that potential issues are identified and addressed in a timely manner. It also requires the ability to engage constructively with investment professionals, maintaining an independent and objective risk perspective.

Main responsibilities

  • Monitor discretionary and advisory portfolios across different asset classes, with a focus on market, credit, liquidity, concentration, counterparty and currency risks.
  • Analyse portfolio composition, diversification and risk exposures, identifying potential weaknesses and deviations from agreed investment strategies.
  • Monitor compliance with investment mandates, investment restrictions and internal limits, investigating and following up on any exceptions.
  • Monitor the activities and performance of External Asset Managers, including portfolio performance, consistency with investment strategies,

portfolio turnover and potential churning, identifying unusual patterns or practices that may not be in clients’ best interests.

  • Review investment positions and transactions independently, challenging Portfolio Managers where necessary on risk exposures and portfolio decisions.
  • Identify and investigate unusual transactions and potential cases of market abuse or other market conduct issues.
  • Prepare investment risk reports for management and risk committees, highlighting relevant exposures, developments and areas requiring attention
  • Contribute to the improvement of risk monitoring methodologies, controls and reporting processes, including the development of tools and the automation of recurring activities.

Do I need the G permit to apply for this role in Ticino as an Italian cross-border worker?

Yes. For EU/EFTA citizens the G permit no longer depends on a border zone: you need residence in an EU/EFTA state, a Swiss contract and a return home at least once a week. The 20 km band matters for taxation, not for the permit. The Swiss employer files the G-permit application at the Ticino cantonal migration office after the contract is signed — first issuance takes 2-6 weeks. The permit lasts five years with an open-ended contract or one over a year, otherwise for the length of the contract.
