Structure Analyst \-

Engineering \& Training Systems

Primary work location

All workplaces

Fly with us into the future and become part of the team at one of the most innovative aircraft manufacturers in the world.

Your Tasks

  • Structural Analysis – Classical static Stress Analysis \& Finite Element analysis of metallic and composite airframe structure with particular emphasis to the Wing and Landing Gear, in both a development and certification\-oriented environment
  • Preparation of stress notes, formal engineering analysis reports, test plans and test reports
  • Additional activities associated directly with CS 23 compliance demonstration
  • Support to structural testing definition and execution

With this you convince us

  • University degree in Aerospace Engineering or a related field (Bachelor's or Master's degree)
  • Minimum of 2 years of post\-graduate experience in structural stress analysis within the aerospace industry
  • Recent graduates holding a Master's degree with an outstanding academic record may also be considered
  • Familiarity with NASTRAN (MSC/NX), FEMAP, or comparable finite element analysis (FEA) software
  • Solid working knowledge of Microsoft Office; experience with Mathcad would be an advantage
  • Knowledge of CS\-23 certification requirements would be advantageous as well as previous experience in wing and landing gear structural analysis would be an Advantage
  • Fluent English skills; proficiency in additional languages is considered an asset

  • Flexible working hours and individual pension solutions
  • Support for training and opportunities for personal and professional development
  • Discounted meals in our staff restaurant and various discounts for employees

Do I need the G permit to apply for this role in Ticino as an Italian cross-border worker?

Yes. For EU/EFTA citizens the G permit no longer depends on a border zone: you need residence in an EU/EFTA state, a Swiss contract and a return home at least once a week. The 20 km band matters for taxation, not for the permit. The Swiss employer files the G-permit application at the Ticino cantonal migration office after the contract is signed — first issuance takes 2-6 weeks. The permit lasts five years with an open-ended contract or one over a year, otherwise for the length of the contract.
