Full description

Build the future of food production – and see the world while doing it.
Do you thrive on challenge, technology, and global adventure? Join a Swiss family-owned company and help commission high-tech production systems for the food and feed industry. Expect up to 80% international travel, working on-site with clients in 3–8 week blocks across the globe. To kick things off, your first year will be based in Prague for onboarding and team integration. Aufgaben: • Manage your projects during commissioning and communicate with important stakeholders around the world (customers, project managers, supervisors) • You will start up the plant or machines with our software • You will be on-site to resolve queries and adjust processes to ensure efficient operation of production lines • Your visit to the customer will also include an assessment of the overall technical condition of the plant and electrical installation • Record important data during commissioning and share it with our department to support continuous improvement processes Anforderungen: • Strong Electrical background • Have practical knowledge and experience in process engineering ideally in the food processing industry • Willingness to learn or experience in commissioning of new machines • Know some basics of PLC programming or willingness to learn • Enthusiasm for travel (> 80% of working time will be spent outside the office on sites) • Professional level of English Two billion people eat food every day that was produced with Bühler equipment. One billion people drive vehicles whose parts were manufactured with our machines. Bühler aims to balance humanity, nature, and the economy in every decision as it develops solutions that unlock sustainable business opportunities in the global food, feed, and mobility industries. We strive to create innovations for a better world, with a special focus on healthy, safe, and sustainable solutions. Therefore, we team up with customers, start-ups, multinationals, and academia to accelerate impact together.
