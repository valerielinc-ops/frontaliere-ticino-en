  • The Kim Laboratory at the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) ( https://www.epfl.ch/labs/upkim/ ) is seeing a highly motivated postdoctoral researcher to investigate how neuronal activity and the physical tumor microenvironment cooperate to drive brain cancer progression. The project
  • Brain tumors do not grow in insulation.
  • Glioma cells form functional connections with neural circuits, response to activity-dependent signals from neurons, and actively remodel the tumor microenvironment surrounding them.
  • Our recent work identified a signaling pathway in which neuron-derived neuroligin-3 engagements CSPG4, increments membrane tension, and activates the mechanical ion channel PIEZO1, thereby promoting glioma proliferation.
  • The successful candidate will build on this work to define the mechanical dialogue between the nervous system and cancer.
  • The project will investigate how tissue stiffness, extracellular matrix composition, mechanosensitive signaling proteins regulatory neuron–glioma interaction and tumor proliferation.
  • The broader goal is to identify therapeutic vulnerabilities that arise from the interaction of tumor cells with their neuronal and physical microenvironment.

Key requirements

  • We welcome applicants with a PhD in neuroscience, cancer biology, cell biology, molecular biology, bioengineering, or a related field, together with excellent writing and Spoken English skills.
  • We are seeing a highly motivated, intellectually curious, and collaborative candidate with strong expertise in whole-cell patch-clamp electrophysiology.
  • Experience with stereostatic surgery, intracranial tumor models, acute brain-slice records, calcium imagining, and cancer research would also be particularly valuable.
